# Experimental, alternative and optional behaviour: the switch register

Reference list of every switch that is off by default, exists for comparison, or can be changed from Settings or a DEBUG launch extra; [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md#switches-and-experiments) explains how they fit together, and [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) holds the evidence.

This is the single register of switches that are off by default or exist for comparison. The rule behind it
(design rule, 2026-10-08/09): an experiment, or the losing arm of an A/B comparison, is kept, off by default and
labelled Experimental, unless it is impossible by first principles. [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) records the evidence
for each.

How to read it:

- DEBUG switches are launch extras named `test_*`; benchmark tunables are in square brackets. Release builds ignore
  them unless an entry names a Settings switch.
- "Control arm" means the default is the new behaviour and the switch restores the old one exactly, so an A/B can
  run in either direction.
- Phones are named by alias: S24, S22, M31, ASUS, Xiaomi, A55 (the reference set) and A15s. "Untested on phones"
  means the entry has unit tests but no measured phone run yet; an entry that cites numbers was measured.
- Entries marked with a Settings name appear under Settings > Advanced.

Promoted features leave the list of experiments. Each has one default constant (`CallFeatureDefaults`, `ConnectivityFeatureDefaults`; privacy features are on by default for user sessions since 0.2.0-beta) and one switch under Settings > Features. `FeatureCatalog` (pure Kotlin) holds each feature's id, preference key, default, benchmark extra and texts, and the Settings card is generated from it. An untouched switch follows the constant, an explicit choice sticks, and a DEBUG benchmark launch (any `test_*` extra) reads only its extras, default off, so case IDs stay valid.

| Viewer frame age: stale-picture notice and "Last update" label (Settings > Features "Show when the picture is out of date"; DEBUG `test_viewer_frame_age` is the benchmark switch) | Promoted 2026-10-11 (default OFF until its phone test passes; one constant, `ViewerFeatureDefaults.FRAME_AGE`) | From an external review of multi-hop media (2026-10-11): with A -> relay B -> viewer C, when B leaves and returns while A keeps sending, C must show an interruption instead of a frozen frame presented as live. Local UI only, no wire change; off, no monitor exists and the Watch screen is unchanged. A picture is stale when no new picture was presented for more than max(1.5 s, 3 frame intervals), or when the parent link was lost and no picture of a newer link has been shown yet. The 1.5 s floor sits just above the largest single Bluetooth burst the adaptive playout delay is built to absorb (0.65-1.3 s scene-cut frames on GATT), so ordinary bursts never flash a warning; three intervals only matter below 2 FPS. Stale shows a light dim over the frozen picture and a badge: "Signal lost - reconnecting" when the link was lost, "Picture stalled - waiting" when the link is up and nothing arrives, plus "Last picture N s ago"; the older centre overlay is not drawn meanwhile and its message becomes the badge's last line. The Watch status chip says "Signal lost" or "Picture stalled" instead of "Watching ...". The first picture of a newer link, or of the same link after a stall, clears everything at once. A broadcaster pause, an ended stream and the app being in the background are never stale (the stall count restarts at the resume). A small label "Last update <1 s ago" (bottom left) shows the age of the last picture. It is counted on this phone and is NOT the camera-to-screen delay: the stream carries only the sender's MediaCodec presentation timestamps (no wall-clock capture time) and no clock relation to this phone exists, so end-to-end age is unknowable here; adding one would need a clock-sync design in PROTOCOL. The clock-free part is logged: both timestamps come from the sender, so newest-received minus presented is how far the picture trails the newest video held (`behindReceivedMs`), and adding the time since that video arrived gives `frameAgeMs`, a lower bound of the real age that excludes everything the sender and relays still hold. Lines (`BENCHMARK ` prefix in DEBUG): once a second `viewer_frame_age: state=live|stale|waiting|paused|ended | cause=none|no_frames|link_lost | sinceFrameMs | frameAgeMs | behindReceivedMs | playoutDelayMs | maxGapMs | thresholdMs | staleEpisodes | recoveries | linkEpoch`; transitions `viewer_stale: event=enter|cause|clear` (clear: `reason=frame|paused|ended|reset`, `staleForMs`, `gapMs` = the whole freeze) and `viewer_link: event=start|playout_base|lost|restored`. Audio and delay on recovery were verified by reading the code, not changed: every link builds a new playout clock at the profile's base delay (`newViewerLinkPlayoutClock`), audio decoder, AudioTrack and video decoder, and closes them when the link ends, so nothing queued or learned on a lost link reaches its replacement (a source or relay also builds a new per-viewer queue for the rejoining viewer, `BluetoothVideoSender` or `RelayChildQueue`, which holds only media offered after the join); `ViewerLinkPlayoutTest` keeps that, and `viewer_link: event=playout_base` shows it per link on phones. Within one link the adaptive delay still grows on late media and shrinks after 60 s calm, by the no-loss design; a stall that keeps the link up therefore replays its backlog late, and `playoutDelayMs`/`behindReceivedMs` measure how much (the bounded-age experiment above is the separate answer). **Phone test (fold into the relay loss/rejoin lane: A -> relay B -> viewer C on CoC and GATT, plus a direct A -> C control; B leaves for 10-30 s by killing its app or switching Bluetooth off, 10 cycles; switch on at C, repeat once with it off), pass when:** (1) `viewer_stale: event=enter` logs at C within about 2 s of the last picture (`sinceFrameMs` at most threshold + 0.5 s), the badge and chip show it, and the frozen frame is never labelled Watching; (2) the cause becomes `link_lost` when C notices the loss (no later than the existing 5-8 s detection) and the badge text matches the cause; (3) the first picture after B returns, or after C re-routes to A, ends it with `event=clear | reason=frame` and a `gapMs` close to the real outage, with no stale state left while pictures keep arriving; (4) over 10 minutes of undisturbed playback on a healthy link no `event=enter` appears except where `viewer_frame_age maxGapMs` really exceeded 1,500 ms; (5) across the 10 cycles every `playout_base` equals the base delay (AUTO 250 ms, a relaying viewer at most its fixed reserve) and `playoutDelayMs` at cycle 10 is not above cycle 1's bound, with `recoveries` equal to the cycles survived; (6) the receiver audio trace shows no audio timestamp older than the first one after the rejoin and none repeated; (7) a 30 s broadcaster pause never shows stale and a stall after the resume counts from the resume; (8) with the switch off the cycle draws nothing and logs no `viewer_*` line. Not yet tested on phones; JVM tests only. |

## Relay and fan-out admission

| Switch | Default | What it does and why it is kept |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relay child re-anchor (`test_relay_child_reanchor=false` to disable) [relay_child_reanchor] | On | A relay whose child link carries under 50 kbps for 2 s with media waiting closes that child's connection so it reconnects on a new anchor (Bluetooth HCI logs showed a child anchor inside the parent's multi-packet window carrying 4-37 kbps for the whole connection, otherwise 349-378). The child retries the same relay first after such a link loss. At most 2 per child per minute, then the withdrawal policy decides. Stream relays only; the private-call relay is unchanged. |
| Relay recovery grace (`test_relay_recovery_grace=false` to disable) [relay_recovery_grace] | On | After a link loss that makes the child prefer its previous relay (the re-anchor above), reconnection waits up to 2 s for that relay's offer instead of taking the first offer heard. Manual test 2026-10-10: the A15s heard the full S24 source 147 ms after the ASUS re-anchor, chose it and waited 15 s without recovering. After 2 s any route is allowed. Stream relays only. |
| Relay cold start (`test_relay_cold_start=false` to disable) [relay_cold_start] | On | A viewer with no relay evidence (relay proven 0, no fresh relay ceiling) counts the incoming link it already carries and may forward to one child. | Relay child stall guard (`test_relay_child_stall_guard=false` to disable) [relay_child_stall_guard] | On (2026-10-11) | Control arm. The tag is read only after the handshake verified it, handed to the notice only from links this phone opened (a stranger who connects to a listener cannot plant a note), kept out of `peerCapabilities`, never put in an advertisement or an end-to-end hello, and unset when the switch is off, in a benchmark launch without `test_newer_versions_nearby`, and for a debug or non-split build (so measured cases keep their exact hello bytes). It is a claim by the peer's identity key, not proof about its APK, so it only decides whether a quiet home note "Newer version nearby (release N)" is shown (`NewerVersionNearby`: newer than installed, at most 20 above, release-key phones only believe release-key claims, dismissal hides for a week, a claim fades after two weeks, nothing about the peer is stored, never during a call or stream). Tapping it opens the update screen's nearby card (github flavour: explains the steps, **Choose the file** copies the picked APK into the private directory and verifies it exactly like a download except that the signer must equal the installed one, then the same hashed PackageInstaller install) or a dialog (F-Droid flavour: update through F-Droid or share the file; nothing is installed by MeshLive). Needs the other phone's Help > Share app nearby or any other way of moving the file. **Phone test (two phones with debug builds, `test_newer_versions_nearby=true`, `test_update_release_number=1` on one and `2` on the other, `test_update_release_key=true` on both), pass when:** after a call and a stream join in each role over CoC, GATT and Classic the older phone shows the home note naming release 2 once the session is over, the newer phone shows none, and `BENCHMARK newer_version_nearby` / `newer_versions_nearby: ... sendsTag=` lines agree; a 0.1.0-beta release build connects to a tagged phone with the same setup success and time as a control run (no `SECURITY`, "Invalid handshake" or "Trailing handshake data" lines on either side); switching off on either phone removes the tag (`sendsTag=null`) and the note; a claim 21 or more above (`test_update_release_number=30`) shows nothing; Hide removes the note until a higher release; a benchmark launch without the extra sends no tag. **Phone test (after the share test passes; github flavour, a newer and an older build signed with the same key):** the newer phone shares through Help > Share app nearby, the older phone receives the file by the browser or any other route and picks it with **Choose the file**; the older phone offers Install with the new version name and Android's confirmation appears; the same flow with a file that is older, signed with another key, a different package, or for the other CPU type ends with the plain-language refusal and nothing installed; the picked file and `noBackupFilesDir/updates/` are empty after an install or a refusal on the next app start.
